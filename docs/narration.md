# Unwrapped: final video narration

Order: intro to camera (~5 s) -> trailer (~1 min, ends on the title shot in
`video/title_shot/unwrapped_title.mp4`) -> fade to black -> explanation with
voiceover (~1:45). Keep music at -20 dB or lower under the voiceover.

## Intro (to camera)

Hi, my name is Lluís, and this is the project I built for the Tripothon.
Hope you like it.

## 1. What it is  [menu, song selected]

This is Unwrapped. It's a video game that teaches kids how music is made.
It's a way to learn while having fun.

I see it as a present: something parents and family can give to a child, and
play together.

## 2. What you learn  [Discover: silence, waking instruments, then the table]

The game has several lessons. First, how a song is built from separate
instruments, called stems. You find each instrument and hear it join the song.

Then mixing: muting and soloing tracks, and audio effects like reverb, delay,
filter and distortion. Each effect shows what it does with a live graph, and
you can hear only what it adds.

And the sound is spatial: where you stand is the mix. If you walk towards the
drums, they get louder. If you move an instrument, its sound moves with it.

(Leave two seconds without talking while walking towards the drums.)

## 3. Why it doesn't get boring  [a mission completing, L through landscapes]

I built it to be interactive, so kids don't get bored. There's a tutorial,
missions with stars, and different game modes, so you can play it again and
again.

## 4. How Tripo is used  [asset board]

Every 3D object in the game is made with Tripo. First, the song is split into
its instruments. Then the program measures how each one sounds: how bright,
how dense, how noisy. That becomes the prompt, and Tripo turns it into a 3D
model. Even the words in the lyrics become objects.

## 5. Your own song  [the upload button and progress bar, cut before it ends]

The idea is that people choose their own songs. You upload a song, the program
separates it into instruments, and Tripo turns them into 3D models. A few
minutes later, your song is a world you can walk into.
